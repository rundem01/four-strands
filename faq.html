Four Strands

Questions

Worth knowing before the first row.

What is Four Strands?

A quiet daily tracker for people who want their faith and their body kept in the same practice. Each day you mark four things, and every full day is woven into a record you can see grow.

What are the four strands?
  • Scripture — a passage read and sat with.
  • Strength — dumbbells, or honest recovery.
  • Food — one focus kept through the day.
  • Sleep — hours held, screens put down.

Mark all four in a day and that day lays a full row in the weave.

Where is my data stored?

On your device, and nowhere else. There are no accounts and no server — your marks live in your browser's storage. The privacy policy has the full picture.

How do I back up my record, or move to a new device?

Use export to download your record as a small file, keep it somewhere safe, and use import on any device to restore it. Do this before clearing your browser or switching phones — local storage doesn't survive either on its own.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After your first visit it caches itself and runs fully offline — marks, the weave, and all the guides.

How do I install it like a normal app?

On iPhone or iPad: open the site in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android: open it in Chrome and tap Install when offered, or choose Add to Home screen from the menu. It then opens full-screen from its own icon.

Which Bible translations does it carry?

The Berean Standard Bible by default, plus the World English Bible and the King James Version. All three are public domain or CC0 and live inside the app, which is part of why it works with no internet.

What happens if I miss a day?

The weave simply shows an open space for that day. Nothing is deleted and nothing resets. The record is honest, but it's patient — the point is coming back tomorrow, not keeping a spotless run.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Four Strands is free, with no ads and no locked features.

Who is it for?

Anyone wanting a simple, private way to hold Scripture, training, eating, and rest as one daily practice — on their own, with a training partner, or across a whole community where faith and fitness meet.
