Why the King James?The free one

The KJV (1611) is fully public domain, which is why this app can carry its verses offline with no strings attached. Modern translations — NIV, ESV, NLT, NASB — are excellent but under copyright, so they can't be bundled here. The trade: you get the most memorized English Bible ever printed, with 400-year-old vocabulary. Where a word puzzles you, that's usually a good place to slow down anyway.

  • Gospels → Psalms → Proverbs is a good first circuit

How to progressThe rule

Run A / B / C on non-consecutive days, three sessions a week. Rest 60–90 seconds between sets. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set with clean form, add weight and drop back to the bottom of the range. That one rule is most of what progress actually is.

If your dumbbells are lightAdapt

Lower the weight over three slow seconds, add reps, shorten rest, or switch to single-arm and single-leg versions. Each raises difficulty without more iron.

General fitness information, not a prescription. If you're carrying an injury, have a heart condition, or you're coming back after a long break, check with a doctor or coach before you start.

MediterraneanBest evidenced

Vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish, olive oil, nuts, with modest meat and dairy. The pattern with the deepest research behind it for heart health and longevity, and loose enough to actually stick with.

High-protein, whole-foodSuits lifting

Protein at every meal — roughly 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight daily if you're training hard — built on unprocessed foods. Pairs directly with the dumbbell work, since muscle you build needs material to build from.

Plant-forwardFlexible

Plants as the default, animal foods as a smaller part rather than a ban. Cheap and high in fibre. Keep an eye on B12, iron, and total protein if you push it toward fully vegetarian.

Lower-carbAppetite control

Cuts sugar and refined starch, leans on protein, fat, and vegetables. Many people find it blunts hunger, which makes eating less feel less like willpower. Energy in hard sessions can dip during the first few weeks.

Time-restricted eatingTiming, not food

Eating inside a set window — eight to ten hours is common — rather than changing what you eat. It works mostly by making overeating inconvenient. A poor fit if it pushes you to skip the meal around training.

What they all shareThe overlap
  • Mostly whole foods, little ultra-processed
  • Protein at each meal
  • Plenty of fibre — vegetables, legumes, fruit
  • Water as the default drink
  • A pattern you can hold for a year, not four weeks

General nutrition information. For diabetes, kidney disease, pregnancy, a history of disordered eating, or medication that interacts with food, work with a doctor or registered dietitian rather than a list like this.

Monophasic7–9 hours

One consolidated block overnight — what almost everyone should default to. Adults generally need seven to nine hours, and the number of people who genuinely function well on far less is very small.

BiphasicCore + nap

A shorter night plus a nap, usually early afternoon. Common across many cultures and perfectly workable if the total still lands in range. Keep naps to about 20 minutes or a full 90, and before mid-afternoon.

ChronotypeLark or owl

Your natural timing is largely genetic. Morning light and consistent hours can shift it an hour or two, but fighting it wholesale rarely holds. Where you have the choice, build the day around it rather than the reverse.

The 90-minute cycle ideaHandle with care

Sleep does move in cycles, but they run roughly 70 to 110 minutes and vary through the night. Timing an alarm to exact multiples is mostly guesswork. Total hours and a consistent schedule matter far more.

Levers that actually move itIn order
  • Same wake time every day, weekends included — this is the big one
  • Daylight in your eyes within an hour of waking
  • Last caffeine 8–10 hours before bed
  • Cool, dark, quiet room
  • A 30-minute wind-down, dim light, no screen in your face
  • Alcohol near bedtime wrecks the second half of the night

If you regularly can't fall or stay asleep, snore heavily, or feel exhausted despite enough hours, raise it with a doctor. Insomnia and sleep apnoea are treatable, and habit tweaks alone won't fix them.
